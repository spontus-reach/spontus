# Grassroots Sports Sponsorship Platform — Research & Strategy Summary

**Date:** 2026-05-18

## The Idea

A marketplace connecting underexposed sports teams (club sports, high school, rec leagues) with brands seeking grassroots exposure. Think "Shopify for sports sponsorships" or "the sponsorship layer for grassroots athletics." No meaningful competitor exists in the US. Sponsoo (European) is the closest comp but is small and hasn't cracked the US market.

---

## Why This Could Work

**The core problem:** There's a massive, fragmented market of teams that want sponsors and brands that want grassroots exposure, but the transaction cost of finding each other is absurdly high relative to the deal size. A club team's PR manager cold-emails companies from Gmail. A regional protein bar brand has no BD team to find 4,000 club organizations. The connective tissue between supply and demand is one overwhelmed volunteer sending email blasts.

**Timing:** D1 NIL blew the doors open on athletes and teams as marketing channels. Meanwhile, DTC brands are drowning in rising Facebook/Instagram CAC. A $500 club team jersey sponsorship seen at 30 tournaments might outperform $500 in Meta ad spend for the right brand.

**Founder-market fit:** Founded by a Cal Poly club swim athlete who watched the team's PR manager land 20 sponsorships through brute-force cold outreach. Lived the problem firsthand.

## Competitive Landscape

### Why NOT D1

- D1 athletic departments have dedicated staff, existing brand relationships, and multi-year multimedia rights deals
- Learfield, Playfly, JMI Sports manage hundreds of D1 programs and do billions in revenue
- NIL space is crowded: INFLCR, Opendorse, NOCAP Sports are well-funded
- D1 is useful as a **narrative reference**, not a target market: "NIL proved sponsorship is democratizing. D1 got infrastructure first. We're bringing it to the other 99%."

### Why Club Teams Are the Wedge

- Teams are organized enough to deliver on sponsorships
- Athletes are old enough to avoid high school compliance headaches
- Parent/fan demographics skew educated and high-income
- Teams are perpetually underfunded and actively seeking sponsors
- No existing infrastructure or competitors serving this level

### Future Expansion Path

Club teams → D2, D3, NAIA, junior colleges → youth/high school (with state-by-state compliance built in)

## Primary Research: Cal Poly Club Swim

### Current State (from PR manager & former president)

**How sponsorships happen now:**
- PR manager emails companies and hopes for the best
- No standardized pricing, no tracking, no repeatable process
- Every deal happens differently: personal connections, cold calls, walking into retail stores, leveraging member networks
- Despite brute-force approach, landed ~20 sponsorships in one year

**Key deals documented (from former club president Kyle, pre-2022):**
- **Clif Bar:** Came through a personal connection from a team member
- **Arena:** Required going through a home coach to find a regional rep → eventually reached west coast rep → secured contract
- **Lululemon:** Former president walked into the downtown store, talked to an associate, got connected to the deals team, negotiated a multi-year team deal
- **J Carroll:** Vendor relationship for printing/embroidery, maintained through weekly phone calls
- **Swim Suits West:** Arena provider, required weekly calls to track orders

**Critical insight — no accountability:** Brands had essentially no requirements for what they received. Clif Bar posts on Instagram stories happened "just to flex," not because they were required. Brands are currently getting zero measurable ROI and are still saying yes.

**Critical insight — knowledge loss:** When the president graduated, all institutional knowledge about sponsor contacts, deal structures, and vendor relationships left with him. Every new officer starts from zero.

## Key Risks & Challenges

### Chicken-and-Egg Problem

Marketplaces live and die by liquidity. Brands show up and see 12 listings in the wrong geography — they bounce. Teams sign up and nobody contacts them for 3 months — they forget the platform exists.

**Mitigation:** Don't launch as a marketplace. Launch as a sponsorship toolkit for club teams. Sign up teams first, help them land sponsors, then flip to a marketplace once you have brand data.

### Small Deal Sizes

$200–$2,000 per deal with 10–20% take = $20–$400 per transaction. This is a pure volume game.

**Mitigation:** Aggregate demand on the brand side. Once you have 500 teams, you're selling network reach, not individual $300 deals. Sponsorship bundles (brand buys exposure across 50 teams in a metro) change the revenue math entirely.

### High School Compliance

State athletic associations have strict, varying rules about sponsorship, commercial activity, and uniform branding. Some states prohibit individual athlete sponsorships entirely.

**Mitigation:** Build compliance into the platform by state. This is annoying but becomes a moat — if you solve it, nobody wants to re-solve it. Start with college club teams to avoid this entirely in the early stage.

### Measurement & ROI

Brands will ask "what did I get for my $500?"

**Mitigation:** Build lightweight measurement: require teams to upload photos of logo placement, report event attendance, track social media posts. Even basic proof of exposure is miles ahead of the current state (which is nothing).

## Go-to-Market Strategy

### Phase 1: Don't Build — Learn (Weeks 1–2)

1. **Debrief current PR manager.** Get full data: how many brands contacted vs. how many said yes (conversion rate), cash vs. product deals, what was promised, what was delivered, which brands might return next season. Look at actual emails if possible.
2. **Talk to 5+ other Cal Poly club team leaders.** Not surveys — real conversations. Key question: "Does your team have sponsors, and how did you get them?" Testing whether the problem generalizes beyond swim.
3. **Leverage the Cal Poly club swim president's connections** to the broader club sports department. One conversation could open the door to every club team on campus.
4. **Talk to 3 brands that already sponsored your swim team.** Ask: why did you say yes? What did you feel you got? Would you do it again? Would you sponsor other club teams if someone brought you the opportunity?

### Phase 2: Manual Matchmaking (Weeks 3–8)

- Manually match 10 teams with brands
- Facilitate deals with templated agreements
- Take a cut
- Close 30–50 sponsorship deals by hand
- Document every friction point, failure mode, and surprise

### Phase 3: Build the MVP

Automate exactly what was done manually. Priorities based on what was painful and repetitive vs. what needed human judgment vs. what nobody cared about.

### Phase 4: Expand

Cal Poly → other UC/CSU club programs → national college club sports → D2/D3/NAIA → youth/high school

## What the Platform Eventually Becomes

Not just matchmaking — the operating system for team sponsorships:

- **Discovery:** Teams and brands find each other
- **Contracting:** Templated agreements, standardized sponsorship tiers
- **Payment:** Handle transactions, take a cut
- **Asset delivery:** Send teams their branded banners/materials
- **Proof of performance:** Photos, social posts, attendance data
- **Renewal:** Automated re-ups each season

Advanced features at scale:
- Sponsorship bundles (brand buys 50 teams in a metro area)
- Category exclusivity
- Dynamic pricing based on team performance or tournament schedule
- Brand database filtered by category and relevance
- Sponsorship tier templates for teams
