# Product Vision

## The Model: LinkedIn for Sponsorships

Brands post sponsorship listings with specific criteria. Verified club teams browse and apply. Platform handles verification, standardized profiles, and deal facilitation.

---

## How It Works

**Brand side:**

1. Brand creates verified company profile
2. Posts a sponsorship listing with specifics: budget (cash or product), target region, sport preference, team size requirements, social media minimums, season/timeframe, what they expect in return (jersey logo, social posts, event presence, etc.)
3. Receives applications from verified teams
4. Reviews standardized team profiles side by side
5. Selects teams, deal is facilitated through platform

**Team side:**

1. Team creates verified profile (must prove status as official club sport RSO)
2. Profile auto-populates with: roster size, sport, location, Instagram followers, competition schedule, past sponsors, photos
3. Browses active sponsorship listings
4. Applies to relevant ones
5. Brand selects them or doesn't

---

## What Actually Works About This Model

**Eliminates the cold email problem.** The single biggest pain point from all research is that teams don't know how to pitch brands and brands don't know which teams are legit. This removes both problems. Brands state what they want. Teams show they fit. No pitch deck needed, no spray-and-pray.

**Two-sided verification builds trust.** Every team on the platform is verified as a real club sport with real athletes and real competition schedules. Every brand is verified as a real company. This is the trust layer that currently doesn't exist anywhere. It's what makes a brand comfortable saying yes to a team they've never heard of.

**Standardized team profiles replace messy emails.** When a brand reviews applicants, they see the same format every time: roster size, followers, events, location, past sponsors. This is dramatically better than reading 50 differently formatted cold emails. Standardization makes comparison possible and decisions faster.

**Scalable without the founder in the middle.** Unlike the manual matchmaking model, this doesn't require someone to personally broker every deal. The platform facilitates discovery and application. Brand chooses. This can scale to thousands of teams and hundreds of brands without linear cost growth. This is the thing that makes it venture-scale.

**Clear revenue model.** Multiple options that all make sense: brands pay to post listings, platform takes a percentage when a listing converts to a signed deal, premium placement for teams who want to stand out, or subscription tiers for brands who want ongoing access to the network.

**"LinkedIn for sponsorships" is a one-sentence pitch anyone understands.** Investors, brands, teams, parents, coaches. No explanation needed.

---

## What's Genuinely Hard About This Model

**Chicken-and-egg is still real.** Brands won't post listings if there are no teams. Teams won't sign up if there are no listings. This is the fundamental marketplace problem and a new UI model doesn't solve it. It just makes the experience better once both sides show up.

**Mitigation:** Seed the platform by reaching out to brands manually, getting their sponsorship criteria, and posting listings on their behalf. Teams see active listings on day one. Can also pre-populate team profiles from public data (Instagram, club websites) and invite teams to claim and verify their profiles.

**Brands may not want to post publicly.** Some brands treat their sponsorship strategy as competitive intelligence. Posting "we want to sponsor 10 club teams in California" tells competitors exactly what they're doing. Some brands prefer to be approached privately.

**Mitigation:** Offer private listings that are only visible to teams matching specific criteria. Or allow brands to browse team profiles and reach out directly (more like LinkedIn recruiter mode).

**Application spam.** If listings are open, every team applies to everything regardless of fit. Brand gets 200 applications for 3 spots. Signal-to-noise ratio drops and brands stop using the platform.

**Mitigation:** Matching criteria filters (auto-reject teams that don't meet minimum follower count, wrong region, wrong sport). Limit number of active applications per team. Quality score based on profile completeness and past sponsorship history.

**Small deal sizes still challenge unit economics.** The LinkedIn model doesn't change the fact that individual deals are $200-$2,000. Take rate on a $300 product deal is still tiny. Need volume or need to push toward bundle deals.

**Mitigation:** Brand-side bundle listings ("sponsor 10 teams for $5,000") where the platform matches automatically. This is where revenue actually scales.

**Verification is operationally heavy at the start.** Manually verifying every team and every brand takes real work. At 50 teams it's manageable. At 5,000 it's a full-time job unless automated.

**Mitigation:** Automate verification through Instagram API, university club directories (like Cal Poly Now), and governing body databases (CollClubSports, CCS, etc.). For brands, verify through business registration or LinkedIn company pages.

---

## What This Model Does NOT Solve (And Still Requires Manual Work)

- Getting initial brands to post listings (still requires outbound sales by the founder)
- Getting initial teams to create profiles (still requires outbound outreach by the founder)
- Contract facilitation and payment processing (needs to be built)
- Proof of performance after deals close (teams uploading photos, social posts, etc.)
- Handling disputes when one side doesn't deliver

---

## How This Connects to the Go-to-Market

The LinkedIn model is the Phase 3/4 product. It does NOT replace the manual work in Phase 1-2.

**Phase 1-2 (now through summer):** Manually facilitate deals. Learn what both sides need. Collect data on deal structures, objections, failure points. Every manual deal teaches you what the platform needs to automate.

**Phase 3 (fall 2026):** Build the MVP platform with this LinkedIn model. Seed it with the teams and brands you've already worked with manually. They become your launch users.

**Phase 4 (winter 2026+):** Expand beyond Cal Poly. Use governing body relationships (CollClubSports, CCS, NIRSA) to onboard teams at scale. Use brand relationships from manual phase to seed listings.

## Features to Build (Priority Order)

### Must Have for MVP

- Team profiles with verification
- Brand profiles with verification
- Sponsorship listing creation (brand side)
- Application flow (team side)
- Basic messaging between matched teams and brands
- Standardized sponsorship agreement templates

### Should Have

- Auto-populated team data from Instagram
- Matching criteria filters on listings
- Payment processing through platform
- Proof of performance uploads (photos, screenshots, reach data)

### Nice to Have (Later)

- AI-generated contract drafts based on both parties' inputs
- Bundle listings (sponsor 10+ teams at once)
- Analytics dashboard for brands (impressions, reach, ROI)
- Category exclusivity options
- Dynamic pricing suggestions based on team metrics
- Brand-initiated outreach (recruiter mode — brand browses teams and reaches out)
