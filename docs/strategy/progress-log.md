- Mapped VC funding landscape in AI sports sponsorship space

### Key Insights

1. Every deal at swim club was free product, not cash. Brands have never been asked for cash.
2. No accountability — brands got zero measurable ROI and were fine with it.
3. Institutional knowledge dies when officers graduate. Every new person starts from zero.
4. Small teams (UC Davis, 20 people) self-select out of sponsorships assuming they're too small.
5. Kyle validated the core product: "what would help is knowing who to contact — the network."
6. TeamSnap's 200-player minimum excludes our entire target market.
7. Nobody is building a verified marketplace for college club sports sponsorships in the US.
8. VC money is flowing into AI sports sponsorship tools — market is validated but window is closing.

### Product Vision Developed

## Day 3 — May 21, 2026

### Jason Laird (Cal Poly Triathlon Sponsorship Coordinator) — CRITICAL RESPONSE

Most detailed and valuable response of the entire project. Tri has the most sophisticated sponsorship operation of any Cal Poly club team.

**Key data points:**
- Tri has 10 active sponsors currently
- Lezyne partnership is dead (ended before Jason joined). Was originally enabled by a CPTri alumni who worked at Lezyne. That same alumni is now a main contact for Fluid Nutrition, one of their favorite sponsors.
- Roka partnership didn't work out as intended. Jason cold outreached Sailfish as a replacement swim equipment/wetsuit sponsor.
- Garmin REACHED OUT TO THEM. A Garmin rep wanted a Zoom about calendar apps, Jason got connected to the sponsorship department, and now Garmin sponsors the team. This proves brand-side demand — brands are actively looking for grassroots teams.
- Sunglasses sponsor was originally with cycling team only. Jason reached out to cycling to get the contact, then brought that brand to tri as well. Cross-team referrals work.

**How their sponsorships are structured:**
- Prioritize long-term partnerships, not one-offs. "It doesn't look good on our part to be hopping around from sponsor to sponsor every year."
- When a brand signs on, their logo goes on kits and cycling apparel for the following year
- Team promotes sponsors on social media "to the best of our capabilities"
- Brands can come sell products at MTS (March Triathlon Series) — tri's hosted event
- Most companies repost tri's social posts on their own stories — mutual social proof

**How they find sponsors:**
- Mix of cold outreach and brands reaching out to them
- Alumni connections are a major channel (Lezyne via alumni, Fluid Nutrition via same alumni)
- Cross-team referrals (sunglasses brand from cycling)
- Personal connections through Zoom calls that lead to sponsorship departments (Garmin)

**Proof of exposure:**
- Mostly trust-based. Brands see social posts, repost on their stories
- "More or less they trust that it's happening, but they can also see the proof"
- No formal reporting or analytics required

**Officer turnover — THEY SOLVED IT:**
- Have a documentation system: notes on what worked, how to maintain relationships
- Run team surveys asking what gear to make cheaper/more accessible
- Jason just sent transition emails to every sponsor introducing the new coordinator, thanking them, and expressing commitment to long-term partnership
- This is the exception, not the norm. Most teams (like swim) lose everything when officers graduate.

**Insights for platform:**
1. Alumni network feature — connect teams with alumni at potential sponsor companies
2. Cross-team brand discovery — if cycling has a sponsor, tri should see it and be able to apply
3. Sponsor relationship continuity — platform holds the relationship so officer turnover doesn't kill it
4. Brands reaching out to teams validates the LinkedIn model — brands WANT to post listings and find teams
5. Long-term partnership preference means retention/renewal features matter
6. Event-based activation (brands selling at MTS) is a sponsorship asset most teams haven't thought of

### Jason Laird Follow-Up Response — Deal Structure Details

**All deals are product/discounts. Zero cash.**

Types of sponsorship deals tri has:
- **Discount codes:** Sailfish provides a general coupon code shared via team Slack
- **Group order accounts:** Carbs, Schwalbe, and Garmin give sponsorship email/password for a general account where Jason can make group orders
- **Free product for events:** Sailfish sent 350 swim caps for MTS athletes
- **Prize sponsorships:** 100% gave free sunglasses for 1st place podium, Voler gave free jersey for 2nd place, Carbs gave $50 gift card for 3rd place
- **On-course support:** Fluid sponsored all on-course nutrition at MTS
- **Service booths:** Cambria Bike had a booth for pre-race bike mechanic help
- **Gift cards:** Running Warehouse gave 24 total gift cards split across podiums
- **Packet stuffing + hosting:** GH Sports filled athlete packets with gels and coupons, hosted packet pickup at their store the day before the race

**MTS (March Triathlon Series) is their sponsorship centerpiece.** 8+ brand activations at one hosted event. This is tri's most valuable sponsorship asset.

**Tiers have collapsed.** There used to be distinct tiers but now all sponsors get the same deliverables: social media shoutouts + option to attend MTS and market their brand. Managing different tiers was too complex manually.

**Brands confirmed active with tri (updated list):**
Sailfish, 100%, Voler, Carbs, Schwalbe, Garmin, Fluid, Cambria Bike, Running Warehouse, GH Sports

**Product design implications:**
1. Deal structure templates need to support: discount codes, group accounts, free product, gift cards, service booths, event activations — not just "cash or product"
2. "Hosted events" should be a major feature on team profiles — teams that host events are dramatically more valuable to brands
3. Tier management is a pain point — platform could auto-assign deliverables based on what brand provides
4. Local businesses (Cambria Bike, GH Sports) are active sponsors alongside national brands — platform needs to serve both
