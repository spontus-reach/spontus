## TeamSnap - Primary US Competitor

### Company Overview

- **Founded:** 2009, Boulder, Colorado
- **Total funding:** ~$52.5M across 9 rounds
- **Valuation:** ~$107M
- **Employees:** ~188 (as of March 2026)
- **Acquired by:** Waud Capital Partners (2021)
- **Users:** 20 million coaches, administrators, players, and parents
- **Core product:** Youth sports management platform (scheduling, rosters, messaging, registration, payments)
- **Sponsorship:** Bolt-on feature, not core product

### How Their Sponsorship Works

- Matches youth sports organizations with national/regional brands
- Brands get in-app advertising: home screen takeovers, in-app banners, push notifications, dedicated emails, web banners
- Teams get cash payments and product distribution
- Free for teams - TeamSnap monetizes through the brand side
- Their team handles fulfillment (printing banners, shipping warm-up shirts)
- Guaranteed minimum sponsorship of $250-$5,000 for new TeamSnap for Business customers
- Brands mentioned in testimonials: DIRECTV, 24 Hour Fitness, Spectrum
- Also offers HundredX fundraising program (parents take surveys to earn $1 each)

### Critical Gaps - Our Opening

**1. 200-player minimum requirement**
TeamSnap requires at least 200 players in a program to qualify for sponsorships, and 500 players for tournaments. Most college club teams have 15-60 members. They literally cannot use TeamSnap's sponsorship feature. This single requirement excludes our entire initial target market.

**2. Youth sports only**
Every piece of their marketing, testimonials, and positioning targets parents of kids in youth sports. College club sports are completely absent from their messaging. Their user base is coaches and parents of minors, not college athletes managing their own teams.

**3. In-app advertising disguised as sponsorship**
TeamSnap sponsorship primarily means brands pay to appear inside the TeamSnap app (banners, home screen ads, push notifications). This is digital advertising, not the kind of real brand partnership our swim team experiences. Arena sending gear, Clif Bar providing product for nationals, Lululemon negotiating a team deal - these are actual sponsorships. TeamSnap sells impressions.

**4. Poor customer service (documented)**
- Email-only support with multi-day response times
- BBB complaints include $20K+ in payments stuck and not distributed
- Not BBB accredited
- Users report being told they are wrong regardless of the problem
- Trustpilot reviews cite aggressive upselling and nonexistent phone support

**5. Sponsorship is a side feature, not the core product**
TeamSnap is a team management app that added sponsorship. They are not iterating aggressively on sponsorship or staffing a dedicated sponsorship product team. We would be building sponsorship as the entire product.

### Our Pitch Against TeamSnap

TeamSnap proved brands want grassroots sports exposure. But their 200-player minimum and in-app ad model leaves millions of smaller teams and all of college club sports completely unserved. We are the sponsorship platform for the teams TeamSnap can't reach.

## Governing Bodies — Potential Distribution Partners

### CollClubSports (National Federation of Collegiate Club Sports Leagues)

- **Founded:** 2000 (originally as National Club Baseball Association)
- **Headquarters:** Pittsburgh, PA
- **Sports governed:** Baseball, softball, football, basketball, track & field
- **Scale:** 399 teams, 7,500+ athletes across five leagues (NCBA, NCSA, NCFA, NCBBA-M, NCBBA-W)
- **What they do:** Organize leagues, run tournaments, provide officials, crown national champions
- **What they DON'T do:** Team-level sponsorship infrastructure
- **Key contact:** Sandy Sanderson (founder)
- **Opportunity:** Their contact page asks "Interested in becoming a sponsor/advertiser?" — they're trying to monetize sponsorships but lack infrastructure. A partnership could give us instant access to 399 organized teams instead of signing them one by one.
- **Pitch:** "You're a league. Sponsorship management isn't your core competency. Let us be your sponsorship platform. We bring brands to your teams, handle contracts, you get a cut."
- **Timing:** Not yet. Need traction first. Phase 4 conversation.

### NIRSA (National Intramural and Recreational Sports Association)

- **Founded:** 1950
- **Scale:** 75+ years operating, serves an estimated 10 million students across campus recreation
- **Sports governed:** Soccer, flag football, basketball, tennis, pickleball championship series. Also oversees intramurals and campus fitness broadly.
- **Partnerships:** USTA, NFL Flag, Association of Pickleball Players, USA Pickleball, Selkirk
- **Brand partnerships:** Already works with Wasserman Next Gen for campus activations connecting brands with college students
- **Sponsorship model:** Sells event-level and organization-level sponsorships (sponsor the NIRSA National Soccer Championship, sponsor a conference, sponsor an institute). Has sponsorship menus for championship series, conferences, regional events.
- **What they DON'T do:** Individual team-level sponsorships. They sell sponsorship of NIRSA events to brands. They are NOT helping the club soccer team at Cal Poly get a protein bar brand on their jerseys.
- **Assessment:** More established than CollClubSports. Already monetizing sponsorships but only at the organizational/event level. Validates that brands want collegiate rec sports exposure. The gap remains: nobody is building team-level sponsorship infrastructure.
- **Relationship to us:** Not a direct competitor. They sell top-down event sponsorship. We build bottom-up team sponsorship. Could eventually be a partner ("NIRSA teams can use our platform for individual team sponsorships") but they already have Wasserman for brand activations so the partnership path is more complex.

### CCS (College Club Swimming)

- Governs collegiate club swimming nationally
- Runs CCS Nationals in Greensboro (the event our Cal Poly swim team gets sponsored for)
- No sponsorship platform or infrastructure for individual teams
- Our founder's direct governing body — potential early partner

### Other Governing Bodies (Fragmented Landscape)

- NCVF — volleyball (hundreds of club teams)
- ACHA — ice hockey
- NCCGA — golf
- USA Ultimate — frisbee
- NCWSA — water skiing/wakeboarding
- USCSA — skiing/snowboarding
- NCFA/ICFF — club football
- NIRCA — running/cross country/track & field
- No single national governing body exists for all club sports
- This fragmentation IS the opportunity: we become the sponsorship layer that sits across all of them

### Key Insight for YC Pitch

"There are 2 million club athletes organized under dozens of fragmented governing bodies. The most established one (NIRSA) only does event-level sponsorship. None of them have team-level sponsorship infrastructure. We're building the layer that sits on top of all of them."

## Additional Competitors & Adjacent Tools (Updated May 2026)

### Sponsa.ai — Brand-Side Sponsorship Management

- **What they are:** A platform that helps multi-location brands centralize sponsorship requests, streamline evaluation, activation and management, and measure community impact
- **Focus:** Youth sports
- **Tagline:** "Youth sports sponsorships made easy — let coaches coach and more kids play"
- **Key distinction:** Built for the BRAND side, not the team side. Solves the problem of a big brand getting 500 inbound sponsorship requests and needing to manage them. NOT a marketplace where teams discover opportunities.
- **Threat level:** Low. Different product category. If anything, our platform generates the inbound that brands would use Sponsa.ai to manage. They're downstream of us.

### Sponsa.io — Sponsorship Link Generator (Australian)

- **What they are:** Australian company founded 2018 in Melbourne. Creates shareable links for individuals selling sponsorship opportunities.
- **Funding:** Pre-seed, 1 investor
- **Scale:** Ranks 189th among 467 competitors in their category
- **Threat level:** None. Tiny, niche tool, not a marketplace.

### TeamLinkt — AI Sponsorship Matching Tool

- **What they are:** Youth sports management platform (similar to TeamSnap) that recently launched an AI Sponsorship Matching Tool
- **How it works:** Uses local business data, industry categorization, and community engagement signals to match youth sports teams with relevant local sponsors
- **Focus:** Youth sports, local businesses
- **Threat level:** Low for now. Youth sports focus, not college club sports. But worth monitoring — they're adding AI-powered sponsorship matching which is conceptually similar to what we'd build.

### OpenSponsorship

- **What they are:** Sports and wellness influencer marketing platform with 25,000+ athletes across 150+ sports and 40+ countries
- **Focus:** Individual athletes as influencers, not team sponsorships
- **Threat level:** Low. Individual athlete focus, influencer marketing model. Not team-level sponsorship.

### Entyx

- **What they are:** AI-driven platform that measures campaign success, calculates engagement, and proves sponsorship ROI for grassroots to pro teams
- **Focus:** Analytics and measurement, not marketplace/discovery
- **Threat level:** Low. Could be complementary — we handle discovery and matching, they handle measurement.

### SponsorUnited

- **What they are:** Leading sponsorship intelligence platform with $45B+ in verified deal data. AI-native operating system for sponsorship.
- **Focus:** Professional sports, major leagues, enterprise brands. Pricing benchmarks, deal data, activation insights.
- **Threat level:** None for our market. Built for pro sports and big brands. Way too expensive and complex for club teams. But validates that sponsorship needs better tooling at every level.

### Sponseasy

- **What they are:** Tool for creating professional online sponsorship proposals. Deck builder with templates, tracking, and PDF export.
- **Focus:** Helping anyone create sponsorship pitch decks
- **Threat level:** Low. A tool, not a marketplace. Could be a feature we build into our platform.

## Funding Landscape: The AI Sports Sponsorship Boom

Venture capital is actively flowing into AI-powered sports sponsorship tools. This validates the market but also means the window to claim the college club sports niche is closing.

### What's Happening

- Platforms like SponsorCX are raising fresh capital from firms like Kickstart to expand automated sponsorship ecosystem tools
- AI-driven youth sports tools have captured attention from major institutional investors including Sony Innovation Fund and Sapphire Sport, leading multi-million dollar rounds
- The amateur/youth sports tech market is being treated as a massive opportunity by VCs
- Legacy sponsorship processes (cold emails, manual outreach, no measurement) are being targeted for disruption across every level of sports

### What This Means For Us

**Positive:**
- We don't have to convince investors the opportunity exists. The money flowing in proves the market is real.
- YC pitch becomes easier: "VCs are pouring millions into AI sports sponsorship, but every funded company targets youth or pro sports. College club sports — 2 million athletes, zero infrastructure — is completely untouched."
- If we get traction, follow-on funding should be available because the category is hot.

**Concerning:**
- The open lane won't stay open forever. If SponsorCX expands downmarket, TeamLinkt's AI matching gains traction, or Sponsa.ai moves into marketplace territory, our niche gets crowded.
- Funded competitors can move fast. A well-capitalized youth sports platform could pivot to college club sports with one product update.
- Every month spent researching instead of executing increases the risk that someone else claims this space first.

### Strategic Implication

This is the strongest argument for speed. The market timing is right NOW. Move fast, claim college club sports, build the network effects that make it hard for a late entrant to displace you. First-mover advantage in a marketplace is real — once teams and brands are on your platform with active relationships, switching costs are high.

## New Competitors Discovered May 21, 2026 — IMPORTANT

### CampusLink (joincampuslink.com) — CLOSEST COMPETITOR

- **What they are:** Marketplace with 100+ brands and 300+ student organizations
- **How it works:** Brands can actively apply to sponsor student organizations, flipping the cold-outreach model. Two-sided marketplace.
- **Focus:** Broad student organizations — not sports-specific. Includes Greek life, academic clubs, cultural orgs, etc.
- **Strength:** Already has supply (300+ orgs) and demand (100+ brands) on the platform
- **Weakness:** Event-centric, not relationship-centric. Sponsoring a single event is different from a season-long jersey partnership. Not club-sports-specific.
- **Threat level:** MEDIUM-HIGH. Closest thing to our LinkedIn model in the US college market. If they add a sports vertical with persistent team profiles and season-long deals, they become a direct competitor.
- **Our differentiation:** Club sports specific, persistent team profiles (not event listings), long-term partnership focus, verified athletic team data (roster, competition schedule, social reach), customizable sponsorship assets (jersey logos vs highlight video placement vs event presence).

### TapSponsor (iOS App, launched ~2025)

- **What they are:** Mobile app for college student orgs to post events and get matched with brands
- **How it works:** Two-way discovery. Student orgs post events (tailgates, formals, philanthropy), brands browse and sponsor specific events.
- **Focus:** Event-based sponsorship matching
- **Weakness:** Purely event-centric. No persistent profiles, no season-long deals, no ongoing relationship management.
- **Threat level:** LOW-MEDIUM. Different model (event-based vs relationship-based). But validates mobile-first approach for college market.

### SponsorMatch (sponsormatch.us, launched 2021)

- **What they are:** General sponsor-seekers marketplace with education, matchmaking, and tools
- **Focus:** Not college-specific. Broad sponsorship marketplace.
- **Threat level:** LOW. Too general, not focused on our niche.

### NIL Club (650,000+ college athletes)

- **What they are:** Platform connecting college athletes with brands for NIL deals
- **Scale:** 650,000+ athletes
- **Focus:** Individual athlete NIL deals, not team-level sponsorships
- **Threat level:** LOW for team sponsorships. But proves massive demand from brands wanting college athlete exposure.

### Updated Threat Assessment

**The competitive landscape is more active than initially thought.** CampusLink is the most concerning — they have a working marketplace with real supply and demand. However, key differences remain:

| What We Do | What CampusLink Does |
|-----------|---------------------|
| Club sports teams specifically | All student organizations broadly |
| Season-long partnerships | Event-based sponsorships |
| Persistent team profiles with athletic data | Event listings |
| Customizable sponsor assets (jerseys, livestreams, banners) | Event presence/activation |
| Long-term relationship management and renewal | One-off transactions |
| Verified athletic teams with competition schedules | General student org verification |

**Strategic implication:** Speed is critical. CampusLink could add a sports vertical. TapSponsor could expand beyond events. The window to own "club sports sponsorships" as a distinct category is open but closing. First-mover advantage in this specific niche matters.

**The positive read:** Multiple companies trying to solve adjacent versions of this problem validates the market. Brands are clearly willing to use platforms to sponsor college organizations. The question is whether a sports-specific, relationship-focused platform can win the club sports niche before a generalist expands into it.
